Job market paper

Charging Towards an Optimal Renewable Energy Transition: The Role of Batteries

Batteries can help unlock a renewable electricity system, but can private investment deliver that transition? I combine an hourly model of grid operations across eight U.S. regions with a growth model in which investment in solar, wind, fossil generation, and batteries responds to economic incentives. In the model’s optimal transition, the social planner builds 12.4 TWh of battery capacity by 2124; without batteries, it builds less than half as much solar. Competitive firms that do not price carbon damages invest too little in the transition, leaving welfare lower by the equivalent of 1.02% of consumption. A carbon tax of $50 per short ton of CO2 closes nearly all of this welfare gap. Subsidies covering solar, wind, and storage together are substantially more effective than subsidizing storage alone.

Working papers

01

Quantifying the Carbon Preserved by Protected Areas in the Amazon

With Francisco Costa, Rafael Araujo, Arthur Bragança, Marcelo Sant’Anna, and Eduardo Adame

Submitted

Abstract

Protected areas (PAs) are among the most important instruments for curbing emissions from deforestation in the Amazon, yet their effectiveness remains debated. In this paper, we evaluate the forest cover and carbon each PA preserves in the Amazon biome. We use an economic land-use model estimated from satellite-based biomass and land-use data. We find that PAs prevent the deforestation of 394,763 km2 and avoid releasing 5.68 billion tCO2 of carbon stored in standing forest—about 16% of global yearly emissions. Three-quarters of the avoided emissions stem from forests that would otherwise have been converted to pasture. Effectiveness varies with accessibility and agricultural potential: PAs closer to markets and with high pasture suitability avert twice as much carbon loss as remote PAs. These results quantify the carbon value of protection and highlight where conservation yields the highest climate gains.

02

Evaluating Rules of Thumb for Vehicle-to-Grid Charge Management

With James Rising

This project studies how electric vehicle fleets can participate in electricity markets through vehicle-to-grid integration. We model charging and discharging decisions that capture energy arbitrage and provide grid regulation services, then compare optimized decisions with a rule-of-thumb charging strategy.

In-class activities

Selected classroom simulations from Banking and Monetary Policy (ECON 308).

01

FOMC game: The Policy Desk

Students take on the roles of monetary policy committee members, respond to economic shocks, and negotiate interest rate decisions. The simulation explores policy tradeoffs, conflicting incentives, and central bank independence.

Activity instructions (PDF) ↗

02

Market for Lemons

A used-car market simulation based on Akerlof’s “Market for Lemons.” Buyers and sellers negotiate under asymmetric information, then explore how certification and inspection change trading outcomes through signaling and screening.

Activity instructions (PDF) ↗

03

Market for Loanable Funds

Students act as banks and firms, negotiating loan amounts and nominal interest rates before inflation is revealed. Using the Fisher equation, they examine real returns, inflation expectations, and the effects of default risk on lending.
